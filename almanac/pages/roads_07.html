Rail Beds and Service Roads

roads · relic page

Page written on the back of an old freight schedule. Times are useless now. The lines are still useful.

The main road belongs to whoever watches it. The side road belongs to whoever remembers it.

Rail beds, powerline cuts, logging roads, drainage paths, old farm lanes, and service roads can move you around trouble. They can also move you into trouble nobody bothered to mark.

  • Rail beds: steady grade, long sight lines, little shade, easy to be seen.
  • Powerline cuts: direct paths, exposed ridges, mud traps, good landmarks.
  • Logging roads: useful until washouts, gates, slides, or snowpack.
  • Farm lanes: may lead to food, dogs, people, or all three.
  • Drainage paths: good in dry weather, deadly in hard rain.
  • Service roads: often connect pumps, towers, tanks, substations, and maintenance sheds.
Smudged note: If grass grows tall in the middle, nobody heavy passed recently.

Watch the ground. Old routes show themselves in two wheel ruts, straight tree lines, gravel where gravel should not be, culverts, fence breaks, and blackberry gaps.

Do not camp in a road cut. It feels hidden until water, rocks, or headlights come through.

The quiet way is not always shorter. It is just quieter. Sometimes that is enough.
