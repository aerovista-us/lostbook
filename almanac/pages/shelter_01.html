Appended notes

Wind & rain first

Shelter is not comfort — it is keeping people dry enough to think and warm enough to sleep. Sketch the prevailing wind before you stake anything.

  • High ground, not the lowest bowl — cold air pools there overnight.
  • Tarp ridge line parallel to wind; doors leeward.
  • Ground cloth inside, not under the whole commune — shared mud is shared illness.
  • Redundancy: one ripped seam should not open the whole sleeping row.

Sleeping row layout

  • Head-to-foot alternation cuts shared breath lanes.
  • Mark “feet only” at the door with tape — boots stay out.
  • One lantern hook per four beds; no open flame in fabric tunnels.

Margin — if tarps sing like drums, guy lines are too tight or wind shifted. Re-stake before dark.
