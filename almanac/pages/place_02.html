Wind & blackouts

place · copied outage scrap

Scrap from Kootenai Electric / Avista storm notes — Inland Northwest wind events. Glued in after the December blow that took lines down east of the lake.

Wind arrives before you finish arguing about whether it is “that bad.”

Panhandle windstorms are not polite. Gusts in the 60–75+ mph range show up in the record. Trees contact lines. Transmission fails. Restoration is measured in days, not hours, on the east side of Lake Coeur d'Alene and out toward Harrison, Plummer, and Bayview.

  • Assume multi-day blackout when major lines are down — not a “wait until morning” event.
  • Stay 30+ feet from downed lines. Treat every wire as live.
  • Do not run generators in garages or near windows — quiet poison still kills.
  • Check cooperative outage maps when you have signal; do not call lines “dead” because your porch light is off.
  • Chainsaw work after storms kills people who skip helmets, gloves, and a second look at tension in the limbs.
Blackout kit minimum: water you can drink without power · headlamp · warm layer · radio · way to charge one phone · cash
Later note: If downtown still has lights, the east shore may not. Do not mock people for preparing when you got lucky on the grid.
