Lake City notes

place · original hand

This copy picked up habits around Coeur d'Alene — the lake city, the river, the passes.

Kootenai County sits in the Idaho Panhandle about 2,150 feet up. Locals call it the Lake City. The book does not care what you call it. It cares what the weather and water actually do.

  • Lake Coeur d'Alene — long glacial lake (~25 mi). Cools hot days. Cold water year-round.
  • Spokane River — ties lake, towns, and low ground together. Respect moving water after storms.
  • Winters — wet, snowy, workable in town (~45 in/yr snow often cited). Not the same as the pass.
  • Summers — dry, warm, low humidity. Fire and smoke become the weather you feel.
  • Frost window — often ~mid-May before trusting garden plant-outs; hard freeze by mid-October is normal.
Local rule: The valley forecast is not the mountain forecast. Check both before you commit.
Margin note: North of the lake runs cooler than downtown in spring — the water warms the basin, not every ridge.

Microclimates are real here: Sherman Ave can be calm while the east side of the lake loses power for days. Do not assume one report covers your whole route.

Local note: Sandpoint — trade always finds a way.

— R. Vale
