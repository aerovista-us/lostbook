Lake City notes

place · original hand

This copy picked up habits around Coeur d'Alene — the lake city, the river, the passes.

Kootenai County sits about 2,150 feet up. Locals call it the lake city. The book doesn't care what you call it. It cares what the water and weather actually do to your plan.

Lake Coeur d'Alene cools hot afternoons and kills hot afternoons if you treat it like a pool. Spokane River ties towns and low ground — respect it after storms. Winters work in town until they don't; the pass is a different country. Summers dry out, smoke arrives from the south like weather you can taste.

Local rule: The valley forecast is not the mountain forecast. Sherman Ave can be calm while the east side of the lake loses power for days.
Margin: North of the lake runs cooler in spring — the water warms the basin, not every ridge.
Sandpoint: trade always finds a way.

— R. Vale
