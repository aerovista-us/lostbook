Route Marks Without Inviting Trouble

roads · original hand

Leave enough for the right person. Not enough for every person.

A good route mark helps someone who already knows to look. A bad route mark invites everyone with eyes.

Big arrows are for emergencies, rescue, children, and places where confusion kills faster than discovery. Quiet marks are for trusted paths.

  • Three small stones beside the trail, not in the trail.
  • A notch on the back side of a post.
  • Two short scratches under a bridge rail.
  • Cloth tied low where brush hides it from the road.
  • A charcoal dot inside a door frame.
  • A bent grass bundle pointing away from danger.
Field rule: Mark for tired friends, not curious strangers.
Later note: Teach the mark before trusting the mark. A secret symbol is only useful if your people remember it.

Do not mark a route all the way to a sleeping place. Stop short. Let the last part belong to memory, landmarks, or a person waiting watch.

If a mark is discovered and copied by the wrong hands, retire it. Do not get sentimental. Symbols are tools. Broken tools cut the user.

Local note: Same blue truck. Three years apart. Different plates.

— R. Vale
