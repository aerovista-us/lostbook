Radio Windows

signals · trade knowledge

Traded two batteries and a dry pair of socks for this from Mara at the river camp. She said the socks were worth more than the batteries. She was probably right.

Do not talk all day. Listen first.

A radio is not only for calling out. It is for learning who else is awake. Most people waste power by begging into static. Better to choose windows.

  • Dawn: people check weather, movement, and overnight trouble.
  • Midday: traders and road groups may report route conditions.
  • Dusk: camps count people, close gates, and warn each other.
  • After storms: damaged groups call for help or report blocked roads.

Say less than you want. Give enough to be useful. Keep enough back to stay safe.

Simple message shape: who · where · what happened · what you need · when you will listen again

Example: “Two adults, one injured. Old mill road, south bridge. Need bandage and transport. Listening again at dusk.”

Later note: Never give full supply counts over open air. Never say you are alone unless the person already knows.

Mara said every radio message is a little door. Open it wide enough for help. Not wide enough for wolves.

Local note: Priest River — last radio heard here. Once. Storm night. No name.
