Mistake #9

We Stayed One Night Too Long

The meadow was perfect — flat, water at the edge, morning sun on the tent. One night became two because nobody wanted to admit the perfect spot was also the obvious spot.

Second morning: tire tracks crossed ours at an angle that meant someone cut in from the north road, not the lake road. Third morning: we were the tracks other people read. Cooler in the grass. Wrapper we didn't leave. The meadow had become a message board.

We left at noon like we had planned it. We hadn't. The useful lesson isn't "never camp in meadows." It's leave while you're still choosing to leave — before the weather, the strangers, or your own fatigue choose for you.

See: Do Not Camp In The Draw · If You're Lost, Stop · Mistake #5 (told too many).
Local note: Bumblebee pullout — ring still there. So are other people's ashes.

— M. · after we read the tracks wrong
