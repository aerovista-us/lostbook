Bleeding: Pressure First

Bleeding: pressure first, tourniquet last

Most bleeding stops if you press hard enough, long enough, and stop peeking to see if you're done. Every peek pulls the clot you were building.

  1. Direct pressure — cleanest cloth, both hands, full weight. Ten minutes is a start, not a victory lap.
  2. Elevate if you can without breaking something worse.
  3. Soaking through? Add more cloth on top. Do not remove the first pad.
  4. Tourniquet — high and tight only when pressure fails, limb is crushed, or you would lose the limb anyway. Write the time on their forehead. Tell the next person the time.
Medic ledger: "Scary" ≠ "arterial." Arterial spurts with the heartbeat. Venous oozes and still kills if you watch instead of press.
