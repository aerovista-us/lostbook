📡 Signals

Three Means Trouble

signals · original hand

If you cannot be found, you are alone twice.

One shout can be anything. Two might be an argument, an echo, or somebody fooling around. Three is different. Three says pay attention.

Three whistle blasts. Three flashes. Three fires in a line. Three knocks on pipe or wood. Three marks scratched where a person would look for them. It does not always mean rescue. It means someone needed to be noticed.

Field rule: If you hear three repeated signals, stop what you are doing and listen before moving.
  • 3 whistle blasts: distress or “come find me.”
  • 3 flashes: distress after dark.
  • 3 fires: distress visible from distance.
  • 3 stones stacked: human trail mark, check nearby.
  • 3 cloth strips: warning, injury, or route marker.
Later note: Do not answer every signal by walking straight toward it. Look first. Listen longer. A person in trouble and a trap can sound the same from far away.

Children learn this one fast. So do tired people. So do people who have been lost once and do not care to repeat the lesson.

Local note: Priest River — last radio heard here.

— R. Vale

Appended notes

Watch & signals

Night noise discipline is kindness. One whistle pattern, one lantern code — teach both on day one.

  • Two-hour shifts, pairs, written handoff on stump log.
  • Whistle: one = gather, two = medic, three = danger / shelter now.
  • Lantern raised still = stay put; circle walk = all clear.
  • No solo walks past outer ring without check-out on board.
