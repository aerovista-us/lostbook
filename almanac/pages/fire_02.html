One Match Is A Promise

When everything is wet, fire isn't a trick — it's a sequence. Skip a step and you burn your last match proving you're brave. Brave people sit cold.

  1. Tinder from inside the wood — under bark, inside a snapped limb, char cloth from the tin. Ground face-up in rain is lying to you.
  2. Bird's nest bigger than pride — thumb-thick fuel only after the nest has flame, not orange smoke you talk yourself into.
  3. Body and hat as windbreak — light the nest, not the log. A match spent on wet bark is a match spent on nothing.
  4. Pencil sticks one at a time — if the nest dies when you add fuel, your fuel was too big or too wet. Start the nest again while you still have matches.

Wet wood hisses. Dry tinder whispers. Learn the difference before dark because dark is when you lie to yourself about hiss.
