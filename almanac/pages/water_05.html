Cold Shock Comes Before You Can Swim

Fall into lake or river water in the Panhandle — even in summer — and your body gasps and locks up before you decide to swim. That first minute is not about strength. It's about not drowning in the first breath.

  • Float on your back. Don't fight the gasp.
  • Get breathing under control before you kick for shore.
  • Wet clothes steal heat ten times faster than air — get dry or get a vapor layer on even if you feel fine.
Crossed out, rewritten: "I'll swim to shore" → I'll float until I can breathe.

— M. · after the dock at Hayden
