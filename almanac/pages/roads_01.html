🛤 Roads

Roads Lie After Storms

roads · original hand

A road on a map is only a promise someone made before the weather.

After a hard storm, do not trust the road just because you can see it. Roads can look open right up until they drop into a washout, a mudslide, a fallen tree, or water moving faster than it has any right to move.

Walk the edge first if you can. Look at the shoulders. Look where water crossed. Look for fresh cracks, sagging asphalt, missing gravel, tilted signs, and places where grass, branches, or trash all point the same direction.

Field rule: If the road looks clean after a dirty storm, ask where the dirt went.
  • Fresh mud over pavement: water crossed recently.
  • Undercut shoulder: road may collapse under weight.
  • Debris wrapped around guardrail: floodwater reached that high.
  • New cracks in a curve: ground may still be moving.
  • Silent creek bed: can still flash back alive above you.
Later note: Never drive through moving water to prove a point. The river does not care about confidence.

The old highways were built for speed. After the lights, speed became a tax. You paid it in fuel, tires, noise, and mistakes.

Slow is not cowardice. Slow is how you notice the bridge is gone before the front wheels do.
